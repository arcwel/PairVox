# PairVox v0.1: the five checks

Decided 2026-10-17. This is the definition of done for v0.1. A change is made only when one of these checks fails, and the check is measured again after the change. The 60-item review list (`2026-10-16-hardening-task-list.md`) is frozen as backlog: an item from it is worked on only when it is the cause of a failing check.

Every check is measured on the installed app, not on unit tests. `scripts/v01-check.py` measures checks 1 and 2 from the app's log (counts and timings only, never what was said).

| # | Check | Passes when | How it is measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Launch | Shortcuts ready within 5 s and the audio engine started | `scripts/v01-check.py` (or `scripts/launch-check.sh`) after a launch |
| 2 | Voice session | On speakers and on earbuds, separately: at least 10 answered turns each, median time from the end of your speech to the first sound at most 3.5 s (the goal is 2 s), no silence over 1 s inside a reply, no screech captured, no corrupt or dropped sentence | `scripts/v01-check.py` after you talk to it |
| 3 | Approvals by voice | `docs/MIC_TEST.md` parts 4 and 5 behave as written: a spoken yes creates a file, a spoken yes does not run `git push`, another project's request is announced at a pause and can be approved in place | You run the steps; the log confirms each answer |
| 4 | Phone | The paired phone connects over Tailscale, shows a request from the Mac and approves it with a tap | You, with the phone |
| 5 | Security gates | `strix-qa` runs, then `security-audit` in full mode; no open High or Critical finding | Run on the installed build; reports in `docs/security/` |

## The latency target

The 2026-10-13 handoff asked for first audio under 2 s after you stop talking. The parts measured since then add up to more than that: PairVox waits 0.65 s of silence to know you are done, transcription takes about 0.4 s, and Claude's first text alone takes about 2.0 s (`docs/DEV_ENVIRONMENT.md`). **Owner decision, 2026-10-17:** 2 s is the goal and 3.5 s, median, is acceptable. The check passes at 3.5 s and reports whether the goal is met.

## What you do at the Mac (about 20 minutes)

Open one project in PairVox, a scratch git repository is best. Then:

1. **Headphones with a microphone, 10 questions.** (Speakers alone cannot be tested: this Mac has no microphone.) Ask ten ordinary questions about the project ("what does this file do", "where is the config read"), one at a time, letting each answer finish.
2. **Earbuds, 10 questions.** Put the earbuds on and make them the input and output in System Settings → Sound. Ten more questions the same way. If you hear a screech, keep going; it is recorded.
3. **Approvals.** Do parts 4 and 5 of `docs/MIC_TEST.md` (a file write approved by "yes", `git push` refused by "yes", and a request from a second project answered at a pause).
4. **Phone.** On the phone, open PairVox and check it connects. In the Mac project, ask for a file edit, then tap Allow on the phone.
5. Tell Claude you are done. It runs `scripts/v01-check.py` and fills in the table below.

## Findings while measuring (2026-10-17)

- Real turns over the wired headset (run 08043fe9): the one turn with a recorded time started sounding 1.3 s after it was sent to Claude; transcription took 0.2 to 0.3 s per turn.
- The app logs the first-sound time only when the answer starts playing before Claude has finished the turn. In two of three turns Claude finished first, so `firstAudioMs` was -1 and `scripts/v01-check.py` cannot count them. Check 2 needs one more log line, written when the first sound actually plays.
- Phone, (a): `LinkHost.speak` exists but nothing in the app calls it, so the iPhone PRD's P0 item 4 ("spoken replies on the phone") was never built. The phone speaks only request prompts.
- Phone, (b): the phone shows a level meter only for its own microphone button; the Mac's live state reaches it only as a status word.
- Voice approvals, (d): at 23:46:13 and 23:47:23 a five-character answer was not one of the approval words, and the rule "anything else while a request waits goes to Claude as feedback" turned it into a denial. A short answer that is neither yes nor no should be asked again, not refused.
- The headset microphone does not hear the Mac Studio speakers (level stayed at the -69 dB floor while `say` played), so the voice session cannot be driven by synthetic speech here; it needs a person.

### Phone retest, 2026-10-18 (run 0fd0b793)

Passed: the project switcher (switched the Mac to Mission Control from the phone, confirmed in the log); an approval by tap with Face ID.

Still failing, from the owner:
1. The phone's voice is robotic: it uses the default iOS system voice.
2. A long gap between a reply's text and its voice on the phone (not measurable from the Mac log; the Mac's own first sound was 4.4 s after the message).
3. "Allow" spoken on the phone did not approve. Cause: the phone matches its own word list exactly, and recognition adds punctuation ("Allow."), so the answer went to the Mac as a message, which can never approve; and the phone listens only after its mic button is tapped.
4. The phone screen is built around the keyboard; it must be built around the voice conversation.
5. No obvious, continuous sign that the microphone is listening.
6. Needed: a mute button, and push-to-talk as an option (off by default).
7. Everything the system says (replies, requests, notices) must reach every open device, and the phone must listen hands-free like the Mac.
